# Cyber and software officer track (default)

This is your track if you're aiming at Air Force **17X** (17D Warfighter Communications / Cyberspace Operations, 17S Cyberspace Effects) or a technical, software-adjacent path like **62E** Developmental Engineer (computer / software / electronics), or a similar non-rated technical board.

Just so we're clear up front: this is **not** a pilot study plan.

## What the AFOQT actually does for you, specifically

Good news first: for a non-rated line officer, the published floors are pretty low:

- Verbal: **15**
- Quantitative: **10**
- Pilot / CSO / ABM: **no minimum at all**

If you're going 17X, you'll apply to a **non-rated** OTS board — the rated composites don't classify you into cyber. That means: don't burn serious study time on Aviation Information, Instrument Comprehension, or Block Counting unless you also want a shot at a rated slot.

So what actually matters for a technical package? Three things:

1. **Quantitative** (AR + MK) — this is your technical signal, the number that says "I can do the math." Floor is 10, but this app's study target is the **70+** range.
2. **Academic Aptitude** (VA + AR + WK + MK + RC) — the general-aptitude number boards look at. Study target: **65+**.
3. **Verbal** (VA + WK + RC) — the quiet one that trips people up. A 14 here can undo a strong Quant score. Floor is 15 — don't get anywhere near it. Study target: **55+**.

Worth saying plainly: these study targets are competitive bars used inside this app. They are **not** official AFSC cut scores. If you want a number specific to your unit, confirm it with the office that owns your package.

## How boards actually pick 17X / software people

The AFOQT is a gate and a quality signal, but it's not the whole story. Boards also weigh:

- Your degree's CIP code: computer science, computer engineering, electrical/electronics, cybersecurity / cyber operations, software engineering, information systems, mathematics, data science / AI, physics, and related interdisciplinary degrees all count.
- Your GPA and transcript — real CS/EE/math coursework beats a vague "tech" major every time.
- Relevant experience: SOC work, cloud, networking, software delivery, exploit/dev, ICS, crypto, data.
- Your clearance trajectory: 17X billets typically need **T5 / TS-SCI**, and a polygraph shows up on some effects and special-access work.
- Your PFA score, commander ranking / DCR, and leadership record.
- For some direct-commission or constructive-service paths: qualifying cyber/software experience listed in the DAFMAN 36-2032 tables for 17D and 17S.

Bottom line: the AFOQT won't rescue a weak transcript. But a strong Quant/Academic score is what keeps you in the technical stack once the board starts ranking non-rated line candidates.

## A historical note that does NOT apply to your score sheet

There was an AFRL study, using an older test form (Form S), that tried to build a research "cyber composite" — weighting Word Knowledge and General Science plus some other subtests — to predict grades in 17D Undergraduate Cyber Training. Interesting, but not relevant to you: that formula is **not** an official Form T composite. Don't go chasing Instrument Comprehension because of that old paper. Your real targets are still MK, AR, and the verbal trio.

## Where to actually spend your study hours

If you've got about 5 hours a week, here's a reasonable split:

| Hours | Where | Why |
|------:|-------|-----|
| 2.0 | Math Knowledge | Feeds both Quant and Academic. The single highest-leverage thing you can study. |
| 1.0 | Arithmetic Reasoning | The other half of Quant — mostly about translating word problems, no calculator. |
| 0.75 | Word Knowledge | Only a 5-minute section, so it's easy to lose points to speed alone. Feeds Verbal + Academic. |
| 0.75 | Verbal Analogies | It's about spotting relationship types, not memorizing vocab lists. |
| 0.5 | Reading Comprehension | Don't let your Verbal score die on the passages. |
| leftover | Physical Science + SJ | PS is cheap technical points if you remember basic physics/chem. SJ isn't part of a composite — it's officer judgment. |

You can skip or just skim: Aviation Information, Instrument Comprehension, Table Reading, Block Counting.

One thing though — on test day, still **answer every item** in those sections anyway. They're free lottery tickets. Just don't let studying them eat into your MK time.

## Your numbers, floor vs. competitive

| Composite | Floor (published) | This app's cyber/software study bar |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Verbal | 15 | 55 |
| Quantitative | 10 | 70 |
| Academic Aptitude | none published | 65 |
| Pilot / CSO / ABM | none for 17X | ignore |

Here's the rule of thumb the technical community uses: a Quant of **70+** is the number that reads as "engineer." A 25 Quant is enough to commission — but it doesn't read as 17S.

## Don't skip Verbal just because you're technical

Here's something easy to overlook: as a cyber officer you'll be writing CONOPS, target folders, commander notes, and incident reports constantly. The test measures your ability to do that with VA, WK, and RC — not with a CCNA. Treat Word Knowledge like the sprint it is (you get about 12 seconds a question). It's a real pattern: a prior-enlisted cyber NCO who figures "I don't need vocab" is exactly how someone ends up with a Verbal 14.

## What this test does NOT measure

Just to keep things in perspective — this test says nothing about:

- Python, Terraform, Zeek, or MITRE ATT&CK
- Packet analysis
- Cloud architecture
- Whether you can hold a TS/SCI clearance

Those live in your résumé, your certifications, and your clearance process — not here. Use this app for the aptitude battery only, and keep your certs and labs on their own separate clock.

## How the coach in this app should treat you (target track = cyber/software)

- Diagnose MK and AR first.
- Then check WK speed.
- Then VA and RC.
- Only bring up the rated subtests (AI, IC, TR, BC) if you ask, or just as "guess and move on."
- It should never suggest you need a Pilot composite of 70 — that's simply not true for 17X.
