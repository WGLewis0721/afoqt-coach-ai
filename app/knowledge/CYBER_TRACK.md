# Cyber and software officer track (default)

This pack is aimed at Air Force **17X** (17D Warfighter Communications / Cyberspace Operations, 17S Cyberspace Effects) and technical software-adjacent officer paths such as **62E** Developmental Engineer (computer / software / electronics) and related non-rated technical boards.

It is **not** a pilot study plan.

## What the AFOQT actually does for this path

Published commissioning floor for non-rated line officers:

- Verbal **15**
- Quantitative **10**
- Pilot / CSO / ABM: **no minimum**

17X applicants are told to apply to a **non-rated** OTS board. Rated composites do not classify you into cyber. Do not spend serious hours on Aviation Information, Instrument Comprehension, or Block Counting unless you also want a rated slot.

What *does* show up on a technical package:

1. **Quantitative** (AR + MK) — the technical signal. Study target: competitive practice in the **70+** range, not the published 10.
2. **Academic Aptitude** (VA + AR + WK + MK + RC) — board-facing general aptitude. Study target **65+**.
3. **Verbal** (VA + WK + RC) — silent killer. A 14 Verbal washes a strong Quant. Study target **55+**. Floor is 15; do not flirt with it.

These study targets are competitive bars used inside this app. They are **not** official AFSC cut scores. Confirm any unit-specific number with the office that owns your package.

## How boards actually pick 17X / software people

AFOQT is a gate and a quality metric. Classification still leans on:

- Degree CIP codes: computer science, computer engineering, electrical/electronics, cybersecurity / cyber operations, software engineering, information systems, mathematics, data science / AI, physics, related interdisciplinary degrees
- GPA and transcript signal (real CS/EE/math hours beat a vague "tech" major)
- Relevant work: SOC, cloud, network, software delivery, exploit/dev, ICS, crypto, data
- Clearance trajectory: 17X billets are typically **T5 / TS-SCI**. Polygraph shows up on some effects and special-access work
- PFA, commander ranking / DCR, leadership record
- For some direct-commission / constructive-service paths: qualifying cyber/software experience listed in DAFMAN 36-2032 tables for 17D and 17S

AFOQT will not substitute for a weak transcript. A strong Quant/Academic file does keep you in the technical stack when the board is ranking non-rated line.

## Historical note (not on your score sheet)

An AFRL study on Form S tried to predict 17D Undergraduate Cyber Training grades with a research "cyber composite" that weighted Word Knowledge and General Science plus other subtests. That formula is **not** an official Form T composite. Do not chase Instrument Comprehension because of that paper. Chase MK, AR, and the verbal trio.

## Time budget (cyber / software)

If you have 5 hours a week:

| Hours | Where | Why |
|------:|-------|-----|
| 2.0 | Math Knowledge | Feeds Quant **and** Academic. Highest leverage on the whole battery. |
| 1.0 | Arithmetic Reasoning | The other half of Quant. Word-problem translation, no calculator. |
| 0.75 | Word Knowledge | 5-minute section. Easy to fail by speed. Feeds Verbal + Academic. |
| 0.75 | Verbal Analogies | Relationship types, not vocab lists. |
| 0.5 | Reading Comprehension | Don't let Verbal die on passages. |
| leftover | Physical Science + SJ | PS is cheap technical fluency. SJ is officer judgment, not a composite. |

Skip or skim: Aviation Information, Instrument Comprehension, Table Reading, Block Counting.

On test day still **answer every item** in those sections. They are free lottery tickets. Do not study them at the expense of MK.

## Competitive vs floor (this app)

| Composite | Floor (published) | This app's cyber/software study bar |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Verbal | 15 | 55 |
| Quantitative | 10 | 70 |
| Academic Aptitude | none published | 65 |
| Pilot / CSO / ABM | none for 17X | ignore |

Technical community rule of thumb: Quant **70+** is the number that looks like an engineer. A 25 Quant commissions. It does not look like 17S.

## Verbal still matters in cyber

You write CONOPS, target folders, commander notes, and incident reports. The test measures that with VA/WK/RC, not with a CCNA. Treat Word Knowledge as a 12-second sprint. A prior-enlisted cyber NCO who "doesn't need vocab" is how Verbal 14 happens.

## What this test does *not* measure

- Python, Terraform, Zeek, or MITRE ATT&CK
- Packet analysis
- Cloud architecture
- Whether you can hold TS/SCI

Those live in your résumé, certifications, and clearance. Use this app only for the aptitude battery. Keep the certs and labs on a separate clock.

## Default coach behavior

When the target track is cyber/software:

- Diagnose MK and AR first
- Then WK speed
- Then VA and RC
- Mention rated subtests only if the user asks, or as "guess and move"
- Never imply a Pilot 70 is required for 17X
