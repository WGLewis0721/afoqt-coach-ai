# Test day and study tactics

## Before the test

- Sleep. This is a genuinely long day, and being tired hits Table Reading and Instrument Comprehension the hardest first.
- Eat protein and slow carbs beforehand. If the site allows water in the holding area (not the testing room itself), bring some.
- Know your address, parking situation, ID requirements, and start time ahead of time. Late means no test.
- No calculator, period. An analog watch with no calculator functions is usually the safe choice to wear. A lot of eAFOQT rooms show an on-screen timer anyway.
- Scratch paper and pencils are provided — use them for Arithmetic Reasoning, Math Knowledge, and tallying up Block Counting neighbors.

## During the test

- Answer every single item. There's no penalty for guessing.
- Pace yourself by section, not by some vague sense of "getting through the whole test."
- On the speed sections (Word Knowledge, Table Reading, Instrument Comprehension, Block Counting): mark an answer and move on. A perfect answer you never got to is worth exactly zero.
- On Arithmetic Reasoning and Math Knowledge: if you've given a question one honest, focused attempt and it's still not clicking, pick your best guess, flag it mentally, and keep moving.
- On Situational Judgment: think in terms of most effective and least effective. Don't pick the "hero" answer that breaks the chain of command, and don't pick the timid answer that quietly hides a safety problem either.
- On the Self-Description Inventory: stay consistent and honest. Don't try to game a personality inventory — validity scales exist in this family of instruments specifically to catch that.

## When you genuinely have no idea — smart guessing

Once you're down to two choices, just pick one and move on. And if you truly have no idea at all:
- **Verbal**: lean toward the plain, precise word over the dramatic-sounding one.
- **Math**: try plugging in an answer choice from the middle of the range first.
- **Aviation**: go with the textbook (PHAK) answer over whatever "hangar talk" you might have heard.
- **Science**: trust the basic definitions and first-principles units.

## A 4-week study calendar (cyber / software track)

**Week 1 — Build your quant foundation**
- Start with a diagnostic mixed quiz covering just VA, AR, WK, MK, and RC.
- Work Math Knowledge formulas daily, 30–45 minutes. No calculator, ever.
- Practice translating AR word problems into a single equation each.

**Week 2 — Add verbal speed on top of quant**
- Keep the daily MK work going.
- Word Knowledge: roots and 20 flashcards a day, timed at 12 seconds each.
- Verbal Analogies: practice the bridge-sentence method on relationship types.
- Run one timed Arithmetic Reasoning set.

**Week 3 — Lock in Academic Aptitude**
- Two timed Reading Comprehension passages a day.
- Mixed Verbal (VA + WK) speed sprints.
- Only revisit your weak MK topics — work from your miss log, not the whole book again.
- Light Physical Science if you have time left over. Not required for 17X.

**Week 4 — Dress rehearsals**
- Run two full sessions that chain VA → AR → WK → MK → RC, back to back, on a real clock.
- On the rated-track sections (AI, IC, TR, BC): plan to guess-and-move on test day. Don't spend this week studying them.
- Light Situational Judgment practice using the core-values filter. Answer the SDI honestly.

**If you're short on time, here's a bare-minimum daily routine (45 minutes):**
1. 20 minutes: Math Knowledge
2. 10 minutes: Arithmetic Reasoning or Word Knowledge
3. 10 minutes: Verbal Analogies or Reading Comprehension
4. 5 minutes: review your miss log

One last note: the rated/spatial material is optional for this track. Only spend time with OATTS on that side of things if you're also chasing a cockpit seat.
