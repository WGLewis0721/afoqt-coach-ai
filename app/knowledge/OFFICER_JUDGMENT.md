# Situational Judgment + Self-Description Inventory

Neither feeds Pilot / CSO / ABM / Verbal / Quant / Academic in the Form T composite recipes used here. They are still on the clock. SJ trains officer judgment. SDI is a personality inventory.

## Situational Judgment

Public descriptions: interpersonal / leadership scenarios. You typically identify the MOST effective and LEAST effective action from a short list.

### Decision filter (in order)
1. Safety of people and the mission. Hide-the-problem is almost always worst.
2. Integrity. Do not lie, fudge reports, or throw a subordinate under the bus to look good.
3. Chain of command. Solve at the lowest level that still owns the problem. Elevate when safety, legality, or mission is at risk.
4. Develop subordinates. Coach in private, praise in public, document pattern problems.
5. Service before self. The comfortable choice for you is often not the best choice for the unit.
6. Avoid extremes: public humiliation, ignoring a lawful order, going around the boss for ego reasons, doing the whole job yourself because "it's faster."

### Patterns
MOST effective often looks like:
- Gather facts first if the scenario is ambiguous
- Address the person directly and professionally
- Offer help / resources
- Inform the supervisor when policy or safety requires it
- Own your mistake

LEAST effective often looks like:
- Gossip
- Ignore
- Publicly blast someone
- Cover it up
- Immediate maximum punishment with no facts
- "Not my job"

Core values as a rubric, not a slogan:
- Integrity First
- Service Before Self
- Excellence In All We Do

## Self-Description Inventory

~240 Likert items, ~45 minutes. Not right/wrong. Do not try to build a fake "perfect officer" persona. Inconsistent answers and socially engineered patterns are easy to detect on inventories of this type.

Practical advice:
- Answer as you actually operate at work, not as you wish you were on a poster.
- Keep energy up. Random clicking at item 200 is how you look unstable.
- Similar items will reappear in different wording. Be consistent.
