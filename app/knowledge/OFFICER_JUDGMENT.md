# Situational Judgment + Self-Description Inventory

Neither of these feeds into the Pilot, CSO, ABM, Verbal, Quantitative, or Academic composites used in this app — but they're still on the clock, and they still matter. Situational Judgment (SJ) is really about your judgment as a future officer. The Self-Description Inventory (SDI) is a personality inventory, not a knowledge test.

## Situational Judgment

From what's publicly known about this section: you'll get short interpersonal or leadership scenarios, and you typically need to pick the MOST effective action and the LEAST effective action from a short list.

### A decision filter, roughly in this order

1. **Safety of people and the mission comes first.** Hiding a problem is almost always the worst option on the list.
2. **Integrity.** Don't lie, don't fudge a report, and don't throw a subordinate under the bus to make yourself look better.
3. **Chain of command.** Solve problems at the lowest level that can actually own them — but escalate when safety, legality, or the mission is genuinely at risk.
4. **Develop your people.** Coach privately, praise publicly, and document patterns of behavior rather than one-off incidents.
5. **Service before self.** The choice that's easiest for you personally is often not the best choice for the unit.
6. **Avoid the extremes** — publicly humiliating someone, ignoring a lawful order, going around your boss out of ego, or trying to do the whole job yourself because "it's just faster."

### Patterns worth recognizing

The MOST effective answer often:
- Gathers the facts first, if the situation is ambiguous.
- Talks to the person directly and professionally.
- Offers help or points to resources.
- Tells the supervisor, when policy or safety actually requires it.
- Owns up to a mistake honestly.

The LEAST effective answer often:
- Spreads gossip.
- Ignores the problem.
- Publicly criticizes someone.
- Covers the whole thing up.
- Jumps to maximum punishment before the facts are in.
- Shrugs it off with "not my job."

A helpful way to think about this section: treat the core values as an actual decision rubric, not just a slogan:
- Integrity First
- Service Before Self
- Excellence In All We Do

## Self-Description Inventory

This one's long — roughly 240 items on a Likert-style scale, about 45 minutes. There's no right or wrong answer here, so don't try to build a fake "perfect officer" persona. These kinds of inventories are specifically built to catch inconsistent or socially-engineered answer patterns — trying to game it usually backfires.

Practical, honest advice:
- Answer based on how you actually operate at work, not how you wish you came across.
- Keep your energy consistent all the way through. Rushing and clicking randomly by item 200 reads as instability, not fatigue.
- The same idea will come back around in different wording later on. Just be consistent with yourself.
