# Quantitative knowledge (AR + MK)

Real talk first: math anxiety is normal, and this is the section people dread most. But almost everything here is really just pattern recognition — a small set of moves that show up again and again. Learn the moves, and the arithmetic itself is usually the easy part.

The ground rules: no calculator, scratch paper only. Together, Arithmetic Reasoning (AR) and Math Knowledge (MK) make your Quantitative composite. MK also quietly feeds Pilot, CSO, ABM, and Academic — so it's the single highest-leverage section on the whole test.

## Arithmetic Reasoning (word problems)

The whole game here is translation: turn the English sentence into ONE equation, then just do the arithmetic.

### Distance, rate, and time

The core formula: `d = r t` (distance = rate × time).

A few situations worth knowing by heart:
- Two things moving the **same direction**: their relative speed is the **difference** between them.
- Two things moving **opposite directions** (toward or away from each other): their relative speed is the **sum** of them.
- A **round trip** with two legs, maybe at different speeds — this is where almost everyone gets tripped up.

Here's the trap: your gut wants to just average the two speeds. Don't. If the times for each leg aren't equal, averaging the speeds gives you the wrong answer. The only formula that always works is:

**Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.**

Always go back to that. Add up all the distance, add up all the time, divide.

### Work rate problems

If person A can finish a job alone in `a` hours, and person B can finish it alone in `b` hours, working together:

`1/a + 1/b = 1/t`

Same idea for pipes filling or draining a tank: add up the fill rates, subtract the drain rates.

### Percent problems

- "Percent of" something: `part = pct/100 × whole`
- Percent change: `(new − old) / old × 100`
- Successive discounts (a classic trap): you multiply the remaining factors — you don't just add the percentages. 20% off, then another 10% off, comes out to `0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72` of the original price, not 30% off.
- Related trap: raising something by a percent and then lowering it by that *same* percent does **not** bring you back to where you started.

### Ratios and proportions

`a/b = c/d` → cross-multiply: `ad = bc`

This one formula covers scaling recipes, reading maps, similar triangles, and unit conversions.

### Mixture problems

`value = amount × concentration`

When you're combining two mixtures into one: `c1 v1 + c2 v2 = cf (v1+v2)`

### Simple interest

`I = P r t` — rate `r` as a decimal, time `t` in years.

If compound interest shows up: `A = P (1+r)^t`

### Unit conversions worth memorizing cold (don't waste test time deriving these)

- 12 in = 1 ft; 3 ft = 1 yd; 5280 ft = 1 mi
- 16 oz = 1 lb
- 4 qt = 1 gal; 2 pt = 1 qt
- 60 s = 1 min; 60 min = 1 hr
- 100 cm = 1 m; 1000 m = 1 km
- 2.54 cm ≈ 1 in (only use this if the problem actually gives it to you)

### Number sense worth having on autopilot

- Even × anything even = even. Odd × odd = odd.
- Remainder problems: divide, keep the remainder — or think of it as modular math if that clicks better.
- Consecutive integers look like: n, n+1, n+2…
- Consecutive even or odd numbers look like: n, n+2, n+4…

## Math Knowledge (the "pure math" section)

### Order of operations

PEMDAS: Parentheses, Exponents, Multiply/Divide (left to right), Add/Subtract (left to right).

### Fractions

- Multiply straight across: numerators together, denominators together.
- Divide: flip the second fraction and multiply.
- Add or subtract: get a common denominator first.
- A mixed number is just whole-number-plus-fraction.

### Decimals and percents

Move the decimal point two places to switch between a percent and a decimal.

### Exponents

- `a^m a^n = a^(m+n)`
- `a^m / a^n = a^(m-n)`
- `(a^m)^n = a^(mn)`
- `a^0 = 1` (as long as a ≠ 0)
- `a^(-n) = 1/a^n`
- `(ab)^n = a^n b^n`

### Radicals (square roots)

- `√(ab) = √a √b`
- `√(a/b) = √a / √b`
- `√a * √a = a`
- To rationalize a denominator with a binomial, multiply by its conjugate.

### Algebra

**Linear equations**: `ax + b = c` — just isolate x.

**Quadratics**: `ax^2 + bx + c = 0`
- Factor it if it's easy to factor.
- Otherwise, the quadratic formula always works: `x = [-b ± √(b^2-4ac)] / (2a)`
- The part under the square root — the discriminant, `b^2-4ac` — tells you what kind of answer to expect: positive means two real roots, zero means one, negative means none.

**FOIL / multiplying binomials**: `(x+a)(x+b) = x^2 + (a+b)x + ab`

**Inequalities**: the one rule everyone forgets — if you multiply or divide by a negative number, you have to flip the inequality sign.

**Absolute value**: `|x| = a` means x = a OR x = −a (as long as a ≥ 0).

**Systems of equations**: solve with substitution or elimination.

### Functions and sequences

- Slope: `m = (y2-y1)/(x2-x1)`
- Point-slope form: `y - y1 = m(x - x1)`
- Slope-intercept form: `y = mx + b`
- Distance between two points: `√[(x2-x1)^2 + (y2-y1)^2]`
- Midpoint: `((x1+x2)/2, (y1+y2)/2)`
- Parallel lines have equal slopes. Perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals of each other.

### Geometry — angles

- A full circle is 360°. A straight line is 180°. A right angle is 90°.
- Vertical angles (across from each other) are equal. Angles next to each other on a line add up to 180°.
- The angles in any triangle always add up to 180°.
- When a line crosses two parallel lines: corresponding angles are equal, alternate interior angles are equal, and consecutive interior angles are supplementary (add up to 180°).

### Geometry — triangles

- Pythagorean theorem (right triangles only): `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`
- Special right triangles worth memorizing:
  - 45-45-90: legs are `x`, hypotenuse is `x√2`
  - 30-60-90: short leg `x`, long leg `x√3`, hypotenuse `2x` — the trap here is mixing up the long leg with the hypotenuse (it's `x√3`, not `2x`).
- Similar triangles: their corresponding sides are proportional.
- Area of a triangle: `A = (1/2) b h`
- Area of an equilateral triangle: `(s^2 √3) / 4`

### Geometry — other shapes

- Rectangle: `A = lw`, `P = 2(l+w)`
- Square: `A = s^2`, `P = 4s`
- Parallelogram: `A = bh`
- Trapezoid: `A = (1/2)(b1+b2)h`
- Circle: circumference `C = 2πr = πd`; area `A = πr^2`
- Arc length: `(θ/360) * 2πr`
- Sector area: `(θ/360) * πr^2`
- Rectangular box: volume `V = lwh`, surface area `SA = 2(lw+lh+wh)`
- Cube: `V = s^3`, `SA = 6s^2`
- Cylinder: `V = πr^2 h`, `SA = 2πr^2 + 2πrh`
- Sphere: `V = (4/3)πr^3`, `SA = 4πr^2`
- Cone: `V = (1/3)πr^2 h`
- Pyramid: `V = (1/3)Bh`

Use π = 3.14 unless the answer choices are left in terms of π — then match that.

### Probability and counting (lighter coverage)

- Probability = favorable outcomes ÷ total outcomes
- Independent events: multiply their probabilities.
- "And" usually means multiply; "or" usually means add (if the events can't both happen at once).

### Statistics (lighter coverage)

- Mean = the average.
- Median = the middle value once everything's in order.
- Mode = whatever shows up most often.
- Range = biggest minus smallest.

## Mental math shortcuts that save time

- 15% of something = 10% of it + 5% of it (find 10% first, that's easy, then half of that gives you the 5%).
- Multiplying by 25: think of it as `n * 100 / 4` — multiply by 100, then divide by 4.
- Squaring a teen number: `(10+a)^2 = 100 + 20a + a^2`.
- Estimate before you calculate exactly — it lets you throw out answer choices that are obviously way off.

## The traps that catch people again and again

- Mixing up area and perimeter.
- Mixing up radius and diameter.
- Forgetting to flip the inequality sign.
- Averaging averages (see the average-speed trap above — same idea).
- Taking a percent of the wrong base number.
- On 30-60-90 triangles: the long leg is `x√3`, not `2x` — that one's the hypotenuse.
