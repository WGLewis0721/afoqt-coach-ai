# Physical Science

First, the facts on the section itself: 20 items, 10 minutes. Public sources don't fully agree on whether this section feeds into the CSO composite — but it's worth studying anyway if CSO is your track. Either way, this is some of the cheapest points on the whole test if you can dust off first-year physics and chemistry.

## Mechanics

- Newton's 1st law: a body at rest, or moving at a constant velocity, keeps doing that unless a net force acts on it.
- Newton's 2nd law: `F = ma` (force = mass × acceleration). Force in newtons, mass in kg, acceleration in m/s².
- Newton's 3rd law: every force has an equal and opposite reaction force.
- Weight: `W = mg`, where g ≈ 9.8 m/s² ≈ 32 ft/s².
- Work: `W = F d cosθ`. Measured in joules.
- Kinetic energy: `KE = (1/2) m v²`
- Gravitational potential energy: `PE = m g h`
- Power: `P = W/t = F v`
- Momentum: `p = m v`. It's conserved in an isolated collision.
- Impulse: `Δp = F Δt`

For constant (uniform) acceleration:
- `v = v0 + a t`
- `d = v0 t + (1/2) a t²`
- `v² = v0² + 2 a d`

Friction: kinetic friction is `f = μk N`; static friction can be as high as `μs N`.
Circular motion: centripetal acceleration `a = v²/r`, centripetal force `F = m v²/r`, always pointing toward the center.

## Simple machines

The classics: lever, pulley, wheel-axle, inclined plane, wedge, screw.

Mechanical advantage is roughly `load ÷ effort` (in the ideal case). On an inclined plane, the effort you need relates to the component of weight along the slope, `mg sinθ`.

## Waves and sound

`v = f λ` (speed = frequency × wavelength).

Sound needs something to travel through — it moves fastest through solids, slower through liquids, slowest through gases, and not at all through a vacuum. Frequency is what you hear as pitch; amplitude is what you hear as loudness. Doppler effect: when a sound source moves toward you, you hear a higher frequency.

## Light

Speed of light in a vacuum: `c = 3 × 10^8 m/s`.

Reflection: the angle coming in equals the angle going out. Refraction: light bends toward the "normal" (an imaginary line perpendicular to the surface) when it enters a slower, denser medium (higher refractive index, n). `n = c / v`.

Lenses: a convex lens converges light — it can magnify things and focus sunlight to a point. A concave lens diverges light.

Remember ROYGBIV for the visible spectrum: red has the longest visible wavelength, violet the shortest. Ultraviolet carries more energy than violet light; infrared carries less energy than red light.

## Electricity

Charge: protons are positive, electrons are negative, neutrons are neutral.

Current: `I = Q/t`, measured in amperes.
Ohm's law: `V = I R`.
Power: `P = I V = I² R = V²/R`.

In a series circuit: the current is the same everywhere, and resistances just add up.
In a parallel circuit: the voltage is the same across each branch, and `1/Req = 1/R1 + 1/R2`.

Magnetism: a moving charge or current creates a magnetic field — use the right-hand rule to find its direction.

## Heat and thermodynamics

Heat always flows from hot to cold. Three ways it travels: conduction (direct contact), convection (moving fluid), and radiation (electromagnetic waves — no contact needed).

When there's no phase change happening: `Q = m c ΔT`.

Phase changes to know: melting/fusion, vaporization, condensation, sublimation, deposition. During an actual phase change, temperature holds steady at a given pressure — it doesn't rise or fall until the change is complete.

Absolute zero is 0 K, which is −273°C. To convert: `K = °C + 273`.

Density = mass ÷ volume. Water is 1 g/cm³. Ice is less dense than liquid water — that's why it floats.

## Chemistry

An atom has a nucleus (protons and neutrons) with electrons around it in shells.

Atomic number (Z) = number of protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons. Isotopes share the same Z but have different numbers of neutrons.

On the periodic table, elements in the same column (group) share similar chemistry. Alkali metals are highly reactive; noble gases are basically inert.

Ionic bonds form by transferring electrons (typically metal + nonmetal). Covalent bonds form by sharing electrons (typically nonmetal + nonmetal).

pH: 7 is neutral, below 7 is acidic, above 7 is basic. Each whole step on the scale means a 10x change in H+ concentration.

Balanced equations: atoms are always conserved — same atoms in, same atoms out.

Oxidation is losing electrons; reduction is gaining electrons. The memory trick is OIL RIG (Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain).

Avogadro's number: 6.02 × 10^23 particles per mole. You get molar mass straight off the periodic table.

States of matter: solids hold their shape and volume, liquids hold their volume but not shape, gases hold neither.

## Earth and atmosphere (lighter coverage)

Atmospheric layers, low to high: troposphere (where weather happens) → stratosphere (contains the ozone layer) → mesosphere → thermosphere.

Greenhouse gases trap outgoing infrared radiation.

Seasons happen because of Earth's axial tilt — not because of how close we are to the sun. Moon phases come from the geometry of the sun, Earth, and moon lining up differently.

## Units — don't mix these up

SI units: m, kg, s, N, J, W, Pa, A, V, Ω

- 1 N = 1 kg·m/s²
- 1 J = 1 N·m
- 1 Pa = 1 N/m²
- Standard atmosphere ≈ 101.3 kPa ≈ 14.7 psi ≈ 29.92 inHg
